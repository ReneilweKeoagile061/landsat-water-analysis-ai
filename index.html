Rock Lithology & Salinity Analysis

Subsurface rock formations directly dictate groundwater potability and yield. Calcretes and fossil marine clays carry high salinity risks, whereas fractured Karoo sandstones deliver fresh, high-yield water.

Regional Lithology & Salinity Risk Matrix

  • 1. Kalahari Sand & Calcrete (0–40m):
    High surface permeability. Near depressions and pans, intense evaporation creates heavy calcrete hardpans and severe TDS salinity (>2,500 mg/L). Must case off shallow calcrete.
  • 2. Karoo Auob & Nossob Sandstones (40–180m):
    Confined regional artesian sandstones of the Stampriet (STAS) basin. High sustainable yield (≥5–20 m³/h), excellent natural filtration, and fresh potable water (<650 mg/L TDS).
  • 3. Dolerite Dykes & Intrusive Sills:
    Impermeable igneous barriers that dam regional subsurface flow. The fractured contact zone is the prime structural target for high-volume strikes.
  • 4. Dwyka Shales (Deep Saline Hazard):
    Marine glacial tillites containing connate fossil salts. High salinity risk (>4,000 mg/L TDS); avoid drilling into basal Dwyka clays.

Matched Actions & Siting Strategy

  • Borehole Siting (Sandy Loam / High TWI): Target deep permeable recharge fractures with low clay capping for reliable yield.
  • Earth Dams & Ponds (High Clay >30%): Dig lined reservoirs in natural depressions where soil prevents seepage.
  • Contour Swales (Slopes 2–5°): Slow runoff to force infiltration and recharge root-zone water tables.
  • Soil Sponge Mulching (Sandy Uplands): Organic mulch to arrest rapid drainage and minimize evaporation.
